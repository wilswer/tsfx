tsfx::reduction

Module matrix 

Source
Expand description

The rank matrices the correlation and noise steps work on: per feature, the missing values filled with the median, then ranks standardized to mean 0 and variance 1. Ranks make both steps independent of scale and keep a few extreme values from dominating. The correlation step uses average ranks (Spearman); the noise step breaks ties at random instead, so that every pure-noise feature, discrete or not, becomes a uniformly random permutation like the shadow features it is compared with. The cost: within a tie block the order is noise, so a discrete feature keeps only the structure of its blocks. A boolean that splits the rows evenly along their neighborhoods still scores far better than the shadows, but a rare flag (a few percent of ones) keeps little of its signal and is likely dropped as noise.

rank_matrix 🔒
The rows.len() × columns.len() matrix of the columns’ values at rows: missing values (NaN) replaced by the median of the others (the mean of the two middle values for an even count; 0.0 if all are missing), then average ranks standardized to mean 0 and population variance 1. A column of equal values becomes all 0.0. Every index in rows must be a row of every column. Columns are processed in parallel.
standardize 🔒
Mean 0 and population variance 1 in place; all 0.0 without variance.
tie_broken_rank_matrix 🔒
Like rank_matrix, but ties are broken at random, from one seed per column (seeds, as many as columns): the ranks of every column are a permutation of 1..=n, standardized. A column of equal values has no ties to break and stays all 0.0 (it then scores NaN: dropped as noise). The noise step works on this matrix.
